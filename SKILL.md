# Apply Underlying Principles

Use this Skill to turn a real decision into a bounded, evidence-calibrated next step—not to recite models. Always read [the evidence and card standard](references/evidence-and-card-standard.md) first, then read only the domain references needed below. Respect each card's evidence grade, causal label, conditions, and misuse warning.

## Select a mode

- **Application (default):** analyze a real decision or situation.
- **Explanation:** explain a named principle: its accurate meaning, mechanism, conditions, counterexamples, misuse, and practical implication.
- **Audit:** assess a proposed claim or candidate principle against the standard's seven curation criteria. Return exactly one of `core`, `conditional`, `watchlist`, or `reject`; do not modify the knowledge base unless the user explicitly asks.

## Route references

- Prediction, evidence, causality, metrics, or project flow: [epistemics and systems](references/epistemics-and-systems.md).
- Money, product, business, career, or investment: [money and business](references/money-and-business.md), usually with relevant epistemic/system cards. For leveraged investing or financed bets, explicitly check M08 and use it if decision-changing.
- Learning, efficiency, procrastination, habits, or workflow: [work and learning](references/work-and-learning.md), plus system cards when flow matters.
- Negotiation, relationships, wellbeing, stress, or life choices: [social and wellbeing](references/social-and-wellbeing.md), plus epistemic/system cards when uncertainty or causality matters.

## Application sequence

1. **Define the problem:** objective, observable success, horizon, stakeholders, and decision owner.
2. **Identify structure:** constraints, bottlenecks, incentives, feedback/delay, reversibility, and failure cost.
3. **Establish a baseline:** base rate/reference class, status quo, and inaction option where relevant.
4. **Select 3–7 models:** only models that can change the diagnosis or choice.
5. **Cross-check:** conflicts, second-order effects, proxy gaming, tail risk, and cognitive bias.
6. **Generate options:** include inaction; state conditions, consequences, reversibility, and downside rather than silently choosing values.
7. **Design a reversible test:** when a test is appropriate, specify a baseline or comparison, one primary measure, feedback window, and predeclared decision/stop rule. Do not force experiments when they are unsafe, unethical, irreversible, or uninformative.
8. **Calibrate:** keep facts, inferences, and unknowns distinct; state confidence and what evidence would change the conclusion.

Answer conditionally when safe. Ask a question only when the missing fact could materially change direction or risk. Base numerical schedules, thresholds, and step sizes on the user's baseline or a current supporting source; otherwise label them provisional examples or hypotheses to test.

## Output and guardrails

Lead with a concise core judgment. Add only useful sections: key models—each stating why it applies here, what it predicts, and its material boundary; interactions/tradeoffs; options and consequences; next test; uncertainty. Avoid knowledge dumping, decorative neuroscience, causal overclaiming, false precision, silent value choices, guarantees, and treating population averages as personal diagnosis. For current or high-stakes medical, legal, financial, or safety claims, verify current authoritative primary sources at decision time and state relevant professional limits.
